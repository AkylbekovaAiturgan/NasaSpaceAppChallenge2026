1. Project Goal

To create an interactive software platform for daily monitoring of astronauts' physical and mental health during long-duration space missions (for example, flights to the Moon or Mars).

The platform helps the crew and doctors on Earth to:

see health indicators in a quick and clear way;
find negative changes early, before they become dangerous;
keep astronauts motivated to look after their health every day.
2. The Problem

During long spaceflights, the human body faces five main hazards:

#	Hazard	What it means
1	Space radiation	Galactic cosmic rays can damage human DNA.
2	Isolation and confinement	The crew lives in a small closed space for a long time.
3	Distance from Earth	Help is far away, and food and medicine are limited.
4	Gravity fields	There is no normal gravity (microgravity in space, about 3/8 of Earth gravity on Mars).
5	Hostile or closed environment	Microbes can change in space. Air quality and lighting are important.

Because of these hazards, astronauts can have:

loss of muscle mass and bone density (up to about 1–1.5% of bone density per month);
heart and blood vessel changes;
vision changes and balance problems (SANS – Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome);
a weaker immune system;
lack of vitamins and nutrients;
bad sleep, stress, anxiety and a worse mood.

The main question of our project: how can we notice these changes early and help the crew to react in time?

3. Research
3.1 How the hazards affect the human body
Muscles and bones. In microgravity the body does not fight Earth's gravity, so muscles and bones lose load and become weaker.
Heart and blood vessels. Fluids move toward the head. The heart and blood vessels must adapt. Possible problems are irregular heartbeat, blood clots and low blood pressure.
Balance and vision. The vestibular system gets wrong signals. Astronauts can feel dizzy. Hand–eye coordination, balance and spatial orientation become worse. Fluid shift can also change the eyes (SANS).
Immune system. Radiation, microgravity, bad sleep and stress can change immune responses. This can cause allergic reactions or wake up "sleeping" viruses in the body.
Nutrition. There is no fresh food on a long mission. Poor food can lead to a lack of vitamins and can make bones, heart, eyes and thinking worse.
Sleep, mood and behavior. Isolation, noise, light and changed day and night cycles reduce sleep quality. Lack of sleep and stress reduce mood, morale and work quality.
3.2 Useful health indicators and what we can measure in space

Good indicators are simple, objective and quick to check.

Area	What we can measure	Why it is useful
Heart and blood	Heart rate, blood pressure, heart rhythm	Shows heart adaptation, stress and possible arrhythmia
Muscles and bones	Exercise time, strength tests, body mass, bone density scans	Shows loss of muscle and bone
Sleep	Sleep duration, sleep quality, wake-ups	Bad sleep affects mood, attention and immunity
Stress and mood	Questionnaires, daily diary, stress level from sensors	Early sign of psychological problems
Eyes and vision	Eye tests, vision checks	Helps to find SANS
Balance and coordination	Simple motor tests (fine motor skills, hand–eye coordination)	Shows vestibular and sensorimotor problems
Nutrition	Food diary, calories, vitamins, body weight	Shows lack of nutrients
Environment	Air quality, lighting, movement, noise	Important factors for crew health
Immune system	Temperature, allergy symptoms, blood or saliva tests	Shows possible infection or immune changes

These measurements can be made with wearable sensors, simple tests on a tablet and regular medical devices on the station.

3.3 Why do astronauts need regular check-ups?
The body changes slowly, so a person may not notice a problem alone.
Early signs are easier to treat than a serious condition.
Doctors on Earth are far away, so the crew needs fast and clear information.
Regular data shows a trend, for example sleep getting worse every week.
The data helps doctors to improve future missions.
3.4 How the hazards change the health indicators
Hazard	Indicators that can change
Microgravity	Lower bone density, weaker muscles, changed heart rate and blood pressure, worse balance, vision changes
Radiation	Immune response, DNA damage, higher long-term health risk
Isolation and confinement	Worse mood, more stress, less social contact, bad sleep
Distance from Earth	Limited food and medicine, slower medical help, more stress
Closed environment	Air quality problems, microbes, changes in lighting and sleep
4. Our Solution
4.1 Daily tracking and well-being diary

Astronauts track physical activity, nutrition, sleep and heart indicators using wearable sensors. They also write a short personal diary about their mood and symptoms every day.

4.2 Smart "traffic light" assessment system

The software automatically compares the indicators with the normal range and gives a status:

Status	Meaning	What the crew does
🟢 Green – Normal	The indicators are in the normal range.	The crew continues the usual plan.
🟡 Yellow – Attention	An indicator is outside the normal range, or it gets worse for several days (for example, bad sleep for a week).	The crew checks it and adjusts the daily plan.
🔴 Red – Critical	A serious change, such as a dangerous heart rhythm, high stress, sudden vision problems or a sharp drop in condition.	The crew acts at once and contacts doctors on Earth.
4.3 Early trend detection

The app builds dynamic charts (for example, a weekly sleep graph). They help the crew and specialists on Earth to see negative trends early, even when the numbers are still in the normal range, and to notice when several indicators get worse together.

4.4 Gamification

Health tracking is designed like a game, with points, levels, achievements and daily challenges. This keeps astronauts motivated during long and isolated missions.

5. How the Solution Answers the Problem
Problem	What the solution does
Loss of muscle and bone	Tracks daily exercise and gives challenges for training
Heart and vision changes	Monitors heart indicators and vision checks; red status for dangerous changes
Poor nutrition	Tracks food, calories and vitamins
Bad sleep	Weekly sleep graph and early yellow warning
Stress and isolation	Daily diary and mood tracking; gamification for motivation
Slow help from Earth	Clear status and trend data for doctors on Earth
Problems that grow slowly	Early trend detection, even inside the normal range
6. Limits and Next Steps
Medical limits. Exact medical limits must be set by medical experts. The numbers in our concept are only examples.
Next steps:
Choose the main indicators for the first version.
Define example normal ranges with the help of NASA open data and medical sources.
Design the data model and the status rules (green, yellow, red).
Prepare a simple prototype or presentation of the solution.
